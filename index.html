About

From algebra to applied machine learning

My PhD is in mathematics, in algebra, and for most of it I was working with logical-algebraic structures that have nothing to do with neural networks. Somewhere in the middle of that I got pulled into machine learning, first through a plant phenomics project that needed someone comfortable with both the math and the coding, and then on my own, through self-supervised learning. Since finishing the PhD I've stayed in research as an elite researcher at the Signal & Image Processing Lab, Shiraz University.

What keeps me interested in SSL is the constraint it puts on you: you can't lean on huge labeled datasets, so you have to think carefully about what structure in the data you're actually exploiting. I've applied that across a few different areas rather than just one: computer vision, transformer-based sequence and language models, world models and non-contrastive representation learning, federated and distributed settings, and computational biology. The biology side gets a bit more attention below because gene expression data is high-dimensional, expensive to label, and often has far more features than patients, which is exactly the setting where careless evaluation gives you numbers that don't survive contact with a new dataset. Most of my recent projects there, on METABRIC, TCGA, and spatial transcriptomics data, are built around getting that evaluation right first and the modeling second.

Day to day, that means Python, PyTorch, and Slurm, with config-driven pipelines so a result can be rerun exactly, not just approximately, months later. I'd rather report a model that's a bit weaker but measured honestly than one that looks better because of a split that leaked information.

Outside of modeling, I've taught Python and mathematics courses, including a workshop I designed and ran myself, and I keep most of my project code public, partly so the evaluation choices I just described are checkable rather than taken on faith. Details and code for individual projects are below, and the full publication record is on Google Scholar.

Research

What I work on

A few threads run through most of my projects. They overlap more often than they stay separate.

Self-supervised learning

Pretext tasks and contrastive objectives (SimCLR, Barlow Twins, masked autoencoding) for learning representations without relying on labeled data, applied to both images and tabular/omics data.

World models & non-contrastive SSL

Non-contrastive joint-embedding methods (Barlow Twins, VICReg, JEPA-style predictive architectures) that learn by predicting missing or future structure in the data rather than by contrasting pairs of samples.

Federated & distributed learning

Extending self-supervised and representation learning to settings where data can't be centralized, with an eye on how statistical heterogeneity across clients affects what a shared model actually learns.

Computer vision & NLP

Image encoders paired with language decoders, attention mechanisms, and transformer architectures, applied to tasks that sit across both fields rather than inside just one, such as image captioning (see Vision2Words).

Bayesian uncertainty quantification

MC dropout and Bayesian deep survival models for cases where knowing how confident a model is matters as much as the prediction itself, particularly in clinical settings.

Machine learning for genomics & healthcare

Working with high-dimensional transcriptomic and histology data (METABRIC, TCGA, spatial transcriptomics), where the main risk is evaluation that looks strong but doesn't hold up out of sample.

Mathematical foundations

A background in algebra and logical-algebraic structures that I still draw on when thinking about what a model is actually learning, rather than only how well it scores.

Projects

Open-source work

All code is public on GitHub. A few include a live, interactive demo you can run in the browser.

Family homelessness early warning

Forecasts which English local authorities will see a sharp rise in family homelessness a year ahead, using only public Open Government Licence statistics. Reports two tasks separately and is explicit about where the model beats a naive baseline and where it doesn't.

pythonforecasting
Live dashboard → Code

Parsimony

A reinforcement learning agent (Rainbow DQN and PPO) that decides which gene expression test to order next for a patient, trading diagnostic accuracy against measurement cost, trained on TCGA pan-cancer data.

rlrainbow-dqnppo
Live demo → Code

Histology–omics fusion

Multimodal fusion of spatial transcriptomics and histology images for predicting cortical layers, using self-supervised pretraining before the fusion step.

multimodalself-supervised
Live demo → Code 🐳 Docker

TCGA cancer type classifier

Classifies five cancer types from RNA-Seq gene expression, comparing logistic regression, random forest, and a small neural network, with a browser dashboard for exploring the data and trying predictions live.

rna-seqclassification
Live demo → Code

Bayesian survival analysis — METABRIC

Bayesian deep survival models with MC dropout on the METABRIC breast cancer cohort, with a focus on evaluating the resulting uncertainty estimates and selective prediction rather than just point accuracy.
